Four sentences, nine years

You can hear a child growing up.

These are the four things children say at Farmhouse, and roughly when they start saying them. Everything else we do is in service of getting from the first to the last.

Age 3

“I can do it myself.”

  • Chooses, carries, pours and puts things back.
  • Stays with something difficult for longer than you might expect.
  • Begins to take responsibility for their own little world.
Age 5

“I can do it myself.”

  • Reads, writes and manages familiar routines with growing confidence.
  • Uses real tools and materials with care.
  • Asks why, and wants to find out.
Age 9

“I can work things out.”

  • Plans work, collaborates and follows through.
  • Explains a point of view — and can change their mind when another idea is stronger.
  • Develops a powerful sense of fairness, responsibility and belonging.
Age 12

“I can take ownership.”

  • Takes an idea from question to finished project.
  • Knows what they don't know — and how to find out.
  • Forms views, speaks with confidence, and can explain the thinking behind them.

Translation, for grown-ups

What they say. What it's actually worth.

The children put it better than we do. Here it is again, in the language of a job description.

“I can do it myself.”→Independence. Starting a task, and finishing it, without being managed.
“Stays with something difficult.”→Concentration and persistence. The thing every teacher after us will want.
“I can work things out.”→Problem solving. Planning work, collaborating, following through.
“Can change their mind.”→Intellectual honesty. Rarer than it should be, at any age.
“Knows what they don't know.”→Knowing how to learn. The only durable skill in a world that keeps changing.
