A completely ordinary Tuesday

So what actually happens all day?

Fair question. Most school websites won't tell you. Here is a Farmhouse day, start to finish, with no jargon in it.

Reviewer note: the daily structure below is indicative only. The strategy deck does not specify a timetable, so these times and routines must be confirmed with the school before this page goes anywhere near a parent.

One day, start to finish

No bells. No rows. Quite a lot of concentration.

It can look relaxed from the doorway. The structure is there — it's just built into the room rather than announced from the front of it.

Arrival

Children put their own things away.

Bags, hats, drink bottles, shoes. It sounds trivial. It is the first piece of the whole thing — the day starts with something they manage themselves.

IndependenceBelonging
Morning

The long work session.

The heart of the day. Children choose work from a thoughtfully prepared environment and stay with it — often far longer than you'd expect. A guide moves between them, stretching a child when they're ready rather than when the timetable says so.

Placeholder image
Late morning

Outside, properly outside.

At the Primary campus that means North Head Sanctuary — bushland, headland and ocean air as part of an ordinary day. Exploring is something children do here, not something they read about.

Placeholder image
Midday

Lunch, laid and cleared by the children.

Pouring, serving, wiping down, putting away. Practical work isn't a break from the learning — for a three-year-old it is the learning, and for a nine-year-old it's how a community gets looked after.

Care of the environmentResponsibility
Afternoon

Making, questioning, following an interest.

Creative and hands-on work, often stretching across several days. This is where an older child takes something from a question to a finished thing they're prepared to stand behind.

Placeholder image
Home time

“What did you do today?” “Nothing.”

You will still get this answer. You'll just start noticing other things instead — the shoes going on unprompted, the pouring without spilling, the argument they can suddenly explain both sides of.
